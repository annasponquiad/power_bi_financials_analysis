# Financial Sales & Profit Analysis | Power BI

## About This Project

This is my first Power BI project as part of my training with **DIO (Digital Innovation One)**, and I really enjoyed putting it together!

Although the project was initially guided, I soon found myself experimenting with different metrics, questioning some of the visualisation choices and exploring ways to make the information clearer and more meaningful.

One thing I'm discovering as I learn Power BI is that creating the charts can be surprisingly intuitive. The more interesting challenge is deciding **which data to use, what questions to ask and how best to communicate the answers**.

This project gave me my first opportunity to explore those decisions while building a three-page financial analysis report.

## What I Wanted to Explore

Using a financial sales dataset, I explored several business questions:

- How do sales and profits change throughout the year?
- Which countries contribute the most profit?
- How are discounts distributed across different countries and products?
- Which customer segments generate the greatest share of profit?
- How do sales, profit and units sold vary geographically?

My aim was to create a report that was not only visually clear but also useful for exploring business performance.

## Exploring the Dashboard

### Page 1 — Sales & Profit Summary

I started with an overview of sales and profitability, using KPI cards and a monthly comparison chart.

The report displays approximately **£92 million in sales and £13 million in profit**, with October and December standing out as particularly strong sales months in the 2014 view.

I wanted this page to be straightforward: a quick overview of the headline figures before exploring the data in more detail.

### Page 2 — Profit vs Discount by Country

This page explores how profit and discounts are distributed geographically.

I experimented with several visualisations, including:

- Profit contribution by country
- Discounts across countries
- Discounts broken down by product
- A geographical view of discounts
- A year slicer for interactive filtering

I particularly enjoyed learning how to group data by different columns and use stacked charts to add another layer of detail.

This was also where I began thinking more carefully about which visualisations communicated the information most effectively.

### Page 3 — Profit, Sales and Units Sold by Country and Segment

The final page explores profitability across customer segments alongside geographical performance.

One finding that caught my attention was the **Government segment accounting for approximately 65% of the profit** shown in the segment analysis.

This made me think about how a business could use such information to investigate which customer groups contribute most to its profitability.

## Tools & Skills Practised

**Microsoft Power BI Desktop**

During this project, I practised:

- Importing and exploring a financial dataset
- Aggregating and grouping data
- Creating and formatting KPI cards
- Building column, stacked column and pie charts
- Creating geographical visualisations
- Using slicers to filter report data
- Formatting labels, values and chart layouts

I also became more comfortable navigating Power BI independently, particularly when the available options differed from those shown in the tutorial.

## What I Learned

My biggest takeaway from this project was that **knowing how to build a chart and knowing which chart to build are two different skills**.

I found the Power BI interface surprisingly intuitive, especially coming from Excel. But I also started recognising that good data analysis requires more than dragging fields onto a canvas.

It involves understanding what the numbers represent, selecting appropriate calculations and presenting the results in a way that helps someone make sense of the information.

I also enjoyed going beyond the tutorial instructions. Rather than reproducing every visual exactly as demonstrated, I experimented with different metrics, grouping options and formatting choices.

I even explored Power BI's mobile layout functionality to make the report easier to view on smaller screens!

## What I'd Improve Next Time

Looking back at my first project, there are already things I'd approach differently:

- **Profitability:** Add a profit margin measure to provide more context than sales and profit totals alone.
- **Metric comparisons:** Be more careful when combining financial values and unit counts, as they use different scales.
- **Visual selection:** Experiment with alternatives to pie charts and maps where direct comparisons might be clearer.
- **Business insights:** Include more written observations explaining what the results could mean for stakeholders.

These are all areas I'm looking forward to developing as I continue learning Power BI, SQL and data analysis.
